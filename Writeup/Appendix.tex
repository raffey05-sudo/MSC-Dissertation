\section{H$\alpha$ Emission Constraints}
\label{app:halpha}

To search for Classical Be-type stars and young stellar objects with circumstellar decretion disks, we conducted a targeted narrow-band H$\alpha$ photometric survey using the LCO 0.4m telescope network \cite{brown_cumbres_2013}. Due to the restricted Field of View (FOV) of the LCO imagers relative to the Gaia sky coverage, 34 of the 70 highly probable \texttt{GaussCluster} astrometric members physically fell within the footprint of the observations.

We utilized an automated aperture photometry pipeline to extract instrumental H$\alpha$ magnitudes for these 34 core members. By plotting the instrumental H$\alpha$ magnitude against the Gaia $G_{RP}$ magnitude, we constructed an H$\alpha$ Diagram for the cluster (Figure~\ref{fig:halpha_tcd}). A 1:1 baseline was derived using the median offset of the cluster members, with a strict statistical boundary set at $3\sigma$ to flag emission-line outliers. 

None of the 34 astrometric members crossed this formal $3\sigma$ emission threshold, remaining statistically indistinguishable from the baseline continuum. While the known peculiar emission-line variable V439 Cyg was recovered as a high-probability astrometric member, it did not exhibit significant emission excess during this observational epoch. This null result is consistent with the highly transient nature of Be-star decretion disks, which are known to periodically dissipate and reform over multi-year timescales \cite{rivinius_classical_2013}. The lack of active, strong circumstellar emission across the astrometric core places a meaningful physical constraint on the current evolutionary state of the cluster's B-type population.

\section{Spatial Verification (King Profiles)}
\label{app:king}

To further validate the spatial structure of the astrometrically recovered members, we analyzed their radial surface density distribution. Open clusters exhibit a dense core surrounded by a diffuse halo, a structure mathematically well-described by an empirical King Profile \cite{king_structure_1962}. By calculating the spatial density of the recovered members in concentric annuli expanding outward from the cluster centroid, we fitted standard King models to both Berkeley 87 and NGC 957 (Figure~\ref{fig:king_profiles}).

The resulting fits show a continuous decline in spatial density from the dense core towards the background field level. The recovered members exhibit a concentrated spatial distribution that is described by an empirical King profile. This provides a spatial consistency check on the astrometric membership selection, although the profile alone does not establish that the recovered population is gravitationally bound or relaxed. Notably, the astrometrically selected members closely trace the inner regions described by the King profile, despite spatial position not being included as an explicit dimension in the final \texttt{GaussCluster} membership model. This agreement provides additional evidence that the astrometrically selected population corresponds to the centrally concentrated component of the cluster, while sources with astrometric properties inconsistent with the fitted cluster distribution are excluded from the member sample. The agreement between the spatial and astrometric distributions therefore supports the interpretation of the recovered population as a cluster component, without requiring the King profile alone to establish its dynamical state.



\begin{figure}[h]
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{King_Profiles.png}
    \caption{Empirical King Profile fits for the recovered spatial populations of Berkeley 87 and NGC 957. The steep decay to zero spatial density is consistent with the pipeline isolating the gravitationally bound inner core.}
    \label{fig:king_profiles}
